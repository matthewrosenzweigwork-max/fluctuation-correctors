\noindent\textbf{Scope and status.} This is a synthesis of the candidate THM--027.
Its complete proof and separate independent audit dispositions are recorded in
the immutable Markdown reports and canonical theorem ledger. The construction
establishes a fixed-$N$ first-derivative prerequisite; it does not establish
an interacting-particle It\^o drift or uniform fluctuation estimates.

\section*{Precise assertion}
Use the unit Haar torus, coefficient-one periodic Riesz potential $g$, and
$K=-\nabla g$. Fix $d\ge3$, $0<s\le d-2$, $N\ge2$, $T<\infty$ and
$0\le\nu\le\nu_*<\infty$. Prescribe time-continuous data
$\mu_t\in C^2$, $u_t\in C^1$ and $f_t\in C^3$, with uniform norms;
$\mu_t$ is a probability density. General unknown mean-field norms are not
asserted. The actual homogeneous Fourier reference and smooth terminal test
from Round005 meet these hypotheses.

The pair generator, source and full inverse are
\begin{align*}
 G_t&=\nu(\Delta_x+\Delta_y)+u_t(x)\cdot\nabla_x+u_t(y)\cdot\nabla_y
       +\frac1N K(x-y)\cdot(\nabla_x-\nabla_y),\\
 J_t&=K(x-y)\cdot(\nabla f_t(x)-\nabla f_t(y)),\\
 \Phi_t&=U_t+\int_t^T S_{t,a}R_a\Phi_a\,da,
 \qquad U_t=\E_{t,x,y}\int_t^T J_a(Z_a)\,da,\qquad R=R_x+R_y.
\end{align*}
Here $S$ is the already constructed noncolliding auxiliary pair evolution and
$\Phi$ the unique bounded Borel terminal-zero full inverse. Its previously
proved Haar $L^2$ estimate alone supplied no derivative.

Fix $1<q<d/2$. Choose a positive smooth off-diagonal weight $w_q(z)$ equal to
$|z|^{-q}$ on an embedded ball and bounded above and below outside that ball.
The new assertion is
\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\left(\|\Phi_t\|_\infty+
       \sup_{x\ne y}\frac{|\nabla_{x,y}\Phi_t(x,y)|}{w_q(x-y)}\right)
 \le C<\infty.
\]
The representative is $C^1$ off the diagonal, its derivatives are jointly Borel,
and they are global weak derivatives. Thus $\sup_t\|\Phi_t\|_{H^1}<\infty$.
The constant may depend on $N,\nu_*,T,d,s,q$ and the displayed data and cutoffs.
It is uniform in the chosen $\nu$ in that interval and may grow rapidly in $N$.

\section*{One-sided growth and weighted occupations}
Locally put $z=x-y$ and $r=|z|$. Then $K(z)=s|z|^{-s-2}z+k(z)$
with smooth odd $k$. Put $k_0=2s/N$. The nonsingular relative drift
is bounded by $L_0r$ for a finite fixed-data constant $L_0$.
The principal pair Jacobian has zero center eigenvalues and difference-space
eigenvalues
\[
 k_0r^{-s-2}\quad\text{transverse},\qquad
 -(s+1)k_0r^{-s-2}\quad\text{radial}.
\]
Its largest eigenvalue is the transverse one. Replacing it by the absolute
matrix norm loses the required range. Choose a nonnegative global function
$\ell$ equal to $k_0r^{-s-2}+L$ near zero and dominating the largest symmetric
drift-Jacobian eigenvalue elsewhere. The flow derivative $A_{t,a}$ then obeys
\[
 \|A_{t,a}\|\le\exp\left(\int_t^a\ell(Z_r)\,dr\right)
\]
along each noncolliding path. This pathwise fact alone does not justify
expectation differentiation.

Choose weights with the exact product property $w_\alpha^p=w_{p\alpha}$:
take $R=1/16$ and a smooth radial cutoff $\chi$ equal to one on $B_R$
and supported in $B_{2R}$, and set
$w_\alpha=\exp(\alpha\chi\log(1/r))$, extended as one outside the chart.
For $p\ge0$ and $\alpha>p$, the inner-ball calculation is
\[
 \frac{(G_t+p\ell)w_\alpha}{w_\alpha}
 \le-(\alpha-p)k_0r^{-s-2}
      +2\nu_*\alpha(\alpha+2-d)_+r^{-2}+\alpha L_0+pL.
\]
Write $b=(\alpha-p)k_0>0$ and $a=2\nu_*\alpha(\alpha+2-d)_+$.
The positive diffusion contribution is controlled by the explicit maximum
\[
 \sup_{v\ge0}\left(av^2-\frac b2v^{s+2}\right)
 =\frac{s}{s+2}a\left(\frac{4a}{(s+2)b}\right)^{2/s},
\]
with value zero when $a=0$. The more singular negative repulsive term is
essential, including in dimension three where the displayed diffusion term
can be positive. Bounded annular cutoff derivatives give a finite specified
$C_{p,\alpha}$ such that
\[
 (G_t+p\ell)w_\alpha\le C_{p,\alpha}w_\alpha
 -\frac b2\mathbf1_{r\le R}r^{-\alpha-s-2}.
\]
The full Markdown proof lists every annular and data constant. They can
already grow like $N^{2/s}$ inside a later time exponential.

Stopped smooth It\^o applied to the nonnegative weighted process gives,
after Fatou for its terminal term and monotone convergence for occupation,
\begin{align*}
 \E e^{p\int_t^{t+h}\ell}w_\alpha(Z_{t+h})
     &\le e^{C_{p,\alpha}h}w_\alpha(z),\\
 \E\int_t^{t+h}e^{p\int_t^a\ell}
       \mathbf1_{r_a\le R}r_a^{-\alpha-s-2}\,da
     &\le\frac2b e^{C_{p,\alpha}h}w_\alpha(z).
\end{align*}
No uniform integrability of stopped weights or zero collision boundary value is
assumed. Apply the level bound for a nonnegative supermartingale at doubled
parameters. Integrating its square-root tail gives
\[
 \E\sup_{t\le a\le t+h}e^{p\int_t^a\ell}w_\alpha(Z_a)
 \le2e^{C_{2p,2\alpha}h/2}w_\alpha(z).
\]

\section*{The expectation derivative is a separate proof step}
For a fixed driving path let $F_m$ be the minimum of $m$ and the maximal
weight $w_1$ along its local trajectory through the horizon, using infinity
at a finite collision lifetime. These truncated functions are locally
Lipschitz in the starting point. Below the truncation level this follows
from one safe smooth cutoff flow. Above it a finite time has already crossed
the level, so $F_m$ is locally constant, even at an exploding start.

The previous maximal estimate bounds $F_m$ and its local weak gradient in
$L^P$ of the starting point and probability, uniformly in $m$, for every
$P>2d$. The gradient is bounded by a constant times
$\sup_a e^{\int_t^a\ell}w_2(Z_a)$. Averaging the line-segment fundamental
theorem over a coordinate ball gives the elementary supremum estimate
\[
 \|v\|_{L^\infty(B_b)}\le C_{b,P,d}
       \bigl(\|v\|_{L^P(B_{2b})}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^P(B_{2b})}\bigr).
\]
Its kernel is $|x-y|^{1-2d}$, whose conjugate-power integral is finite because
$P>2d$. Fubini and Fatou bound a subsequence of these random norms almost
surely. Monotonicity of $F_m$ then bounds the untruncated path weight on the
smaller initial ball. No start in that ball can explode. A countable cover
yields a simultaneous local flow for each fixed initial time and horizon;
its image of a compact initial ball stays uniformly separated from collisions.
This common-flow conclusion is derived, not imported.

For a pathwise $C^1$ functional $H(z,W)$, a uniform local bound
$\sup_z\E|D_zH|^p<\infty$ with $p>1$ bounds finite differences by the
line-segment formula and Jensen. Their tails are at most a constant times
$K^{1-p}$, giving uniform integrability. This proves
$D\E H=\E DH$ and continuity of the derivative. The weights above provide
exactly those bounds for propagated terminal functions.

For the singular source, direct differentiation gives
$|\nabla J|\le C_Jr^{-s-1}$ near zero, with bounded remainder. Choose
$\alpha>p$ so that $\alpha+s+2\ge p(s+1)$. The retained negative occupation
term bounds the $p$th moment of
$\int_t^T\|A_{t,a}\||\nabla J_a(Z_a)|\,da$.
For example $p=2$, $\alpha=2(s+2)+1$ suffice for uniform integrability.
Thus the actual signed source potential satisfies
\[
 \nabla U_t=\E\int_t^T A_{t,a}^{\mathsf T}\nabla J_a(Z_a)\,da,
 \qquad
 \sup_t\sup_E\frac{|\nabla U_t|}{w_q}
 \le C_J e^{C_{1,q}T}
       \left(T+\frac{2}{(q-1)k_0}\right).
\]
The zero-terminal derivative is included. The previously proved bounded
source-potential estimate supplies its separate zeroth-order norm.

\section*{Both responses and the full inverse}
The exact first-slot response is
\[
 R_xF=-\int\mu(x+w)F(x+w,y)D(dw)
      -\int K(w)\cdot\nabla\mu(x+w)F(x+w,y)\,dw,
 \qquad D=\operatorname{div}K.
\]
For $0<a,q<d$, splitting near the two separate singular points and their
complement proves
\[
 \int \chi(w)|w|^{-a}w_q(w+z)\,dw\le C_{a,q}w_q(z),\qquad z\ne0.
\]
The convolution need not remain bounded when $a+q\ge d$; the weighted bound
uses $a<d$ and $q<d$ separately. It applies to the force and, below Coulomb,
the divergence density. At Coulomb retain exactly
$D=c_d(\delta_0-dw)$; the atom samples the present off-diagonal point.

A bounded $C^1$ function with gradient bounded by $w_q$ has global weak
first derivatives: deletion of a diagonal tube has boundary contribution
$O(\|F\|_\infty\delta^{d-1})$, while its gradient is integrable. Differentiate
the translated products in the response formula in this weak sense. Fubini,
the convolution bound and the $C^2$ density norms control every term.
Continuity off the diagonal follows by separating the two singular points;
near the moving second point a change of variable fixes it before dominated
convergence. The second response is treated in its own slot. Thus for
$\|F\|_{\X_q}=\|F\|_\infty+\sup|\nabla F|/w_q$,
\[
 \|R_tF\|_{\X_q}\le C_{R,q}\|F\|_{\X_q},\qquad
 \|S_{t,a}F\|_{\X_q}\le e^{C_{1,q}(a-t)}\|F\|_{\X_q}.
\]
Every density-gradient and second-density-derivative term is retained.

Write $(VF)_t=\int_t^T S_{t,a}R_aF_a\,da$ and
$B_q^*=\sup_t\|U_t\|_{\X_q}<\infty$, as proved by the source estimates.
The ordered Volterra series then has bounds
\[
 \|(V^kU)_t\|_{\X_q}\le B_q^* e^{C_{1,q}(T-t)}
       \frac{[C_{R,q}(T-t)]^k}{k!}.
\]
It converges uniformly in values and locally uniformly in derivatives,
without a separable weighted-sup-space assumption. Bounded Borel uniqueness
identifies its sum with the genuine full inverse. Finally $2q<d$ gives
\[
 \sup_t\|\Phi_t\|_{H^1}^2\le C^2\left(1+\int w_{2q}\right)<\infty.
\]
No diagonal trace follows. The crude internal-drift majorant is
$r^{-s-1-q}$, which is not integrable at Coulomb for $q>1$.
Second/time derivatives or a directional cancellation, the exact interacting
particle It\^o identity and uniform evolved residual/bracket estimates remain
separate open gates.
