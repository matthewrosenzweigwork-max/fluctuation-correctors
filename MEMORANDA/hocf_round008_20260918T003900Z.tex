\section*{The precise domain assertion}
Use the unit Haar torus, $K=-\nabla g$, and the coefficient-one periodic Riesz
potential. Fix $d\ge3$, $0<s\le d-2$, $N\ge2$, $T<\infty$, and
$0\le\nu\le\nu_*<\infty$. There is no external drift, and the reference density
is one. A smooth real terminal test $h$ determines the actual homogeneous
backward test
\[
 f_t(x)=\widehat h(0)+\sum_{k\ne0}\widehat h(k)
 e^{-(T-t)[4\pi^2\nu|k|^2+4\pi^2c_{d,s}|k|^{s+2-d}]}
 e^{2\pi i k\cdot x},\qquad
 c_{d,s}=\frac{\pi^{s-d/2}\Gamma((d-s)/2)}{\Gamma(s/2)}.
\]
All its fixed-order spatial and time derivatives are bounded uniformly on the
specified diffusivity interval. Set $E=\{(x,y):x\ne y\}$ and
\begin{align*}
 Bv&=K(x-y)\cdot(\nabla_x-\nabla_y)v,&G&=\nu\Delta_{x,y}+B/N,\\
 J_t(x,y)&=K(x-y)\cdot(\nabla f_t(x)-\nabla f_t(y)),&R&=R_x+R_y.
\end{align*}
The exact homogeneous response is
\[
 R_xv(x,y)=-\int v(x+w,y)D(\dd w),\qquad
 D=\operatorname{div}K=
 \begin{cases}
 s(d-2-s)g_{s+2}(w)\dd w,&s<d-2,\\
 (d-2)|\mathbb S^{d-1}|(\delta_0-\dd w),&s=d-2.
 \end{cases}
\]
The second slot is retained by exchange. Let $\Phi$ be the previously constructed
unique bounded Borel terminal-zero full inverse, with source $J$ and both
responses. It is not replaced by a transport diagnostic or the raw potential.

For every $1<q_1<d/2$ and $q_1+1<q_2<d$, the submitted domain assertion is
\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\left(\|\Phi_t\|_\infty+
 \sup_E\frac{|\nabla\Phi_t|}{w_{q_1}}+
 \sup_E\frac{|D^2\Phi_t|}{w_{q_2}}+
 \sup_E\frac{|\partial_t\Phi_t|}{w_s}\right)<\infty.
\]
Here $w_a(x-y)$ is a fixed positive smooth weight off zero, equal to
$|x-y|^{-a}$ near zero and bounded elsewhere. The indicated derivatives are
jointly continuous off the diagonal, with one-sided time derivatives at the
endpoints. Constants can depend severely on $N$, but are uniform in the chosen
$\nu$. The same representative belongs to global Haar $H^1\cap W^{2,1}$;
$\partial_t\Phi$ and $B\Phi$ belong uniformly in time to Haar $L^1$.

\section*{Higher derivatives require their own occupation bounds}
The local force is $K(z)=s|z|^{-s-2}z+k(z)$ with $k$ smooth and odd. For the
pair flow, the largest singular drift-Jacobian eigenvalue is
$c(z)=2s|z|^{-s-2}/N$; the radial eigenvalue is $-(s+1)c(z)$.
After a fixed cutoff, a finite $L$ bounds the smooth remainder, and the first
variation satisfies $|A_t|\le e^{Lt+I_t}$, where $I_t=\int_0^tc(Z_a)\dd a$.
For every moment order $m\ge0$ and weight exponent $\alpha>m$,
\[
 (G+mc)w_\alpha\le C_{\alpha,m}w_\alpha
 -b_{\alpha,m}\chi|z|^{-\alpha-s-2},\qquad
 b_{\alpha,m}=\frac{s(\alpha-m)}N>0.
\]
The positive term $2\nu\alpha(\alpha+2-d)_+|z|^{-2}$ is absorbed by the more
singular repulsion, including dimension three. The complete memorandum gives
the maximization and all annular constants. Smooth stopping and nonnegative
Fatou passage yield weighted terminal and occupation estimates. A maximal
supermartingale tail, applied at larger moments, supplies every finite moment
on compact sets of initial states.

The expectation derivative is not inferred from pointwise noncollision.
Truncated maximal inverse-distance functions of the initial state are locally
Lipschitz. Their high Sobolev moments, an explicitly proved local Morrey bound
in dimension $2d$, and monotone convergence yield a common noncolliding flow on
each compact initial set. First and second chain rules then have moments above
one. Their pathwise line identities, Fubini and uniform integrability justify
both expectation derivatives and their local continuity.

The second variation is
\[
 H_t=\int_0^t A_{t,r}\,D^2F(Z_r)[A_r,A_r]\dd r,
 \qquad F=N^{-1}(K,-K).
\]
Condition at the intermediate time $r$ to bound the future first variation
against $w_{q_1}$. The remaining occupation has moment order two and source
power $s+3+q_1$. The exact inequality
\[
 s+3+q_1<q_2+s+2,\qquad q_2>2,
\]
allows the $q_2$ barrier to control it. This proves the weighted Hessian estimate
for terminal data and, separately, the integrated singular source. It does not
import a second-derivative conclusion from the first-gradient theorem.

Both homogeneous responses preserve these weighted derivatives. The needed
convolution estimate is
\[
 \int w_a(w)w_q(w+z)\dd w\le C_{a,q}w_q(z),\qquad a,q<d,
\]
with the two singular neighborhoods treated separately. No condition
$a+q<d$ is imposed. At Coulomb the atom and compensation replace an invalid

\section*{Time differentiation and the internal-drift cancellation}
A separate weighted Borel space with norm $\sup_E|v|/w_s$ supports the base
semigroup and the bounded response. An absolutely convergent pointwise Dyson
series defines the full autonomous evolution $\mathcal T_r$. Local continuity
on the specific continuous weighted inputs follows from common-flow moments
and uniform integrability. Strong continuity on the entire weighted supremum
space is neither needed nor asserted.

The source is twice differentiable in time in that weighted norm. Therefore
ordinary differentiation of the time integral gives
\begin{align*}
 \Phi_t&=\int_0^{T-t}\mathcal T_rJ_{t+r}\dd r,\\
 \partial_t\Phi_t&=-\mathcal T_{T-t}J_T+
          \int_0^{T-t}\mathcal T_r\partial_tJ_{t+r}\dd r.
\end{align*}
This differentiates the input and endpoint, not a semigroup generator on an
unjustified domain. Comparing the established base-pair martingale with
localized classical It\^o gives
\[
 \partial_t\Phi+\nu\Delta_{x,y}\Phi+B\Phi/N+R\Phi=-J
 \quad\hbox{on }[0,T]\times E.
\]
Consequently
\[
 B\Phi=-N\bigl(J+\partial_t\Phi+\nu\Delta_{x,y}\Phi+R\Phi\bigr).
\]
Each right-hand term has an integrable majorant: $w_s,w_s,w_{q_2}$ and a
constant. This structural identity supplies the $L^1$ internal drift.
The crude force-times-gradient majorant is generally not integrable at
Coulomb and is not used for this repeated-pair term.

Deleting a diagonal tube in weak integration by parts gives boundary errors
of orders $\varepsilon^{d-1}$ and $\varepsilon^{d-1-q_1}$.
They vanish because $\Phi$ is bounded and $q_1<d-1$.
The conditions $2q_1<d$ and $q_2<d$ give $H^1$ and $W^{2,1}$.
Moving-tube estimates justify differentiated background integrals, including
their continuous representatives and the vanishing integrated background
Laplacian. No diagonal trace is assigned.

\section*{The actual finite-particle identity}
Let $\eta=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{X_i}$ and $\rho=\eta-\dd x$. For a symmetric pair
kernel, write $q(x)=\int\Phi(x,y)\dd y$ and $r=\int\Phi\dd x\dd y$. Then
\[
 P[\Phi]=\frac1{2N^2}\sum_{i\ne j}\Phi(X_i,X_j)
       -\frac1N\sum_iq(X_i)+\frac12r.
\]
For a symmetric triple $F$, the exact ordered deleted-label statistic is
\[
 U_3[F]=\frac1{N^3}\sum_{i,j,k\ \mathrm{distinct}}F(X_i,X_j,X_k)
 -\frac3{N^2}\sum_{i\ne j}F_1(X_i,X_j)
 +\frac3N\sum_iF_2(X_i)-F_0,
\]
Here $F_1$ and $F_2$ integrate one and two Haar coordinates, respectively,
and $F_0$ is the full three-coordinate Haar integral.
At $N=2$ the first sum is empty, but the background terms remain.
Define
\[
 C\Phi=\operatorname{Sym}_3[K(x-z)\cdot\nabla_x\Phi(x,y)],
 \qquad (B\Phi)_\mu(x)=\int B\Phi(x,y)\dd y,
\]
where $\operatorname{Sym}_3$ averages all six permutations. Explicit finite sums
separate the repeated internal pair from three distinct labels before any
estimate. They give
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 dP_t[\Phi_t]=\left\{-P_t[J_t]+U_{3,t}[C\Phi_t]
 +\frac1N\rho_t[(B\Phi_t)_\mu]
 +\frac1{2N}\int B\Phi_t\dd x\dd y\right\}\dd t+\dd M^2_t.
 \quad}
\]
The gradient and its noise are exactly
\begin{align*}
 \nabla_iP[\Phi]&=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{j\ne i}\nabla_1\Phi(X_i,X_j)
       -\frac1N\int\nabla_1\Phi(X_i,y)\dd y,\\
 dM^2_t&=\sqrt{2\nu}\sum_i\nabla_iP_t[\Phi_t](X_t)\cdot\dd W_i(t),&
 d\langle M^2\rangle_t&=2\nu\sum_i|\nabla_iP_t[\Phi_t](X_t)|^2\dd t.
\end{align*}
There is no extra thermal trace: distinct particle labels have independent
Brownian noises, and integrated Haar Laplacians vanish.

For a bounded initial $N$-body density $F_0$, independent of the noises,
the established actual-law bound is
$\|F_t\|_\infty\le\|F_0\|_\infty e^{(N-1)\kappa T}$,
where $D\ge-\kappa\dd x$. Triple terms are Haar integrable because, conditional
on one coordinate, the force and gradient use two independent integration
variables with separately integrable powers. Repeated pairs instead use the
structural $B\Phi$ bound. All drift terms have finite expected absolute time
integrals, and $H^1$ supplies a finite expected bracket at fixed $N$.
The stopped drift passes in $L^1$ and the stopped stochastic integral in $L^2$;
the latter is a true square-integrable martingale. This is a direct collision
localization argument, not a claim of heat-regularized source convergence.
