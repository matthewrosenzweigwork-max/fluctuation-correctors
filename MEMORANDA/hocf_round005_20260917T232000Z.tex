\section*{The precise operator and assumptions}
The torus has Haar mass one and characters $e^{2\pi i k\cdot x}$. Fix
$d\ge3$, $0<s\le d-2$, $N\ge2$, $T<\infty$, and
$0\le\nu\le\nu_*<\infty$. The frozen periodic Riesz potential has
\[
 \widehat g_s(k)=c_{d,s}|k|^{s-d},\qquad
 c_{d,s}=\pi^{s-d/2}\frac{\Gamma((d-s)/2)}{\Gamma(s/2)}
 \quad(k\ne0),\qquad \widehat g_s(0)=0,
\]
and $K=-\nabla g_s$. Prescribe a time-continuous $C^1$ probability density
$\mu_t$, a time-continuous uniformly $C^1$ velocity $u_t$, and a time-continuous
uniformly $C^2$ test $f_t$. Write
\[
 M_0=\sup_t\norm{\mu_t}_\infty,\quad
 M_1=\sup_t\norm{\nabla\mu_t}_\infty,\quad
 \operatorname{div}u_t\ge-D_u.
\]
All stated norms are uniform on the parameter family in question. Their validity
for a general unknown mean-field solution is a separate assertion.

The base pair generator and actual prescribed source are
\begin{align*}
 G_{N,t}&=\nu(\Delta_x+\Delta_y)+u_t(x)\cdot\nabla_x+u_t(y)\cdot\nabla_y
       +\frac1N K(x-y)\cdot(\nabla_x-\nabla_y),\\
 J_t(x,y)&=K(x-y)\cdot\bigl(\nabla f_t(x)-\nabla f_t(y)\bigr).
\end{align*}
The full pair operator is $G_{N,t}+\R_{x,t}+\R_{y,t}$. Both responses have
coefficient one. Symmetry means commutation with exchanging $x,y$; no Haar
self-adjointness is asserted.

\section*{The base potential and the singular passage}
Set $p=s+2$ and
\[
 F_{N,\tau}(r)=\frac N4\left[\left(r^p+\frac{2sp\tau}{N}\right)^{2/p}-r^2\right].
\]
For the local relative generator $2\nu\Delta+(2s/N)r^{-s-2}z\cdot\nabla$,
the radial comparison uses
\[
 \Delta F_{N,\tau}=\frac N2\left[q^{s/p}(d+s-sq)-d\right]\le0,
 \qquad q=\frac{r^p}{r^p+2sp\tau/N}.
\]
The sign holds on precisely the declared range $s\le d-2$. In an embedded
torus ball, write $K(z)=s|z|^{-s-2}z+k(z)$, where $k$ is smooth and odd.
The remaining relative drift has size at most $L|z|$, and
$-r\partial_rF\le sF$. For a fixed radial cutoff $\chi$ supported in that ball,
the product rule retains the diffusion cross term
$4\nu\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla F$. All cutoff errors are bounded by a constant
times $\tau$, uniformly for $N\ge2$ and $\nu\le\nu_*$. Consequently constants
$\alpha,C_B$ depending only on the prescribed data give the barrier
\[
 B_N(t,x,y)=e^{\alpha(T-t)}
 \left[\norm{D^2f}_{\infty}\chi(x-y)F_{N,T-t}(|x-y|)+C_B(T-t)\right],
 \qquad (\partial_t+G_{N,t})B_N\le-|J_t|.
\]
The cutoff is defined in the embedded chart, never by differentiating torus
distance across its cut locus.

Local Picard constructions are patched before collisions. The Lyapunov function
$1+\chi(r)r^{-(d-2)}$ has generator bounded above by a constant times itself.
Stopped expectation therefore bounds the probability of hitting
a tube of radius $\varepsilon$ by a constant times $\varepsilon^{d-2}$ for
each fixed starting point. Compactness excludes other explosion. The global
off-diagonal process has deterministic-time Markov conditioning. Stopped
It\^o applied to $B_N$, followed by monotone convergence for the nonnegative
occupation integral, proves
\[
 \E_{t,x,y}\int_t^T|J_a(X_a,Y_a)|\,da\le B_N(t,x,y).
\]
This establishes integrability before defining the signed potential
$U_N(t)=\E\int_t^T J_a\,da$. It is bounded Borel for each fixed $N$ and is
the unique terminal-zero bounded Borel martingale potential in the stated class.
Radial integration of the barrier, including the finite-$N$ cases where its
core exceeds the cutoff radius, yields
\[
 \sup_t\norm{U_N(t)}_{L^2(\T^{2d})}^2\le C\rho_N,\qquad
 \rho_N=\begin{cases}
 1,&2s<d,\\
 1+\log N,&2s=d,\\
 N^{(2s-d)/(s+2)},&2s>d.
 \end{cases}
\]

The distribution $D_K=\operatorname{div}K$ is a finite signed measure with
$D_K\ge-\kappa$. Choose $\kappa$ at least as large as the periodic remainder
constant $C_0$ in THM--023. Heat regularization preserves this lower bound. The smooth
pair drift has divergence at least $-2D_u-2\kappa/N$; its random-flow Jacobian,
change of variables and Jensen's inequality give
\[
 \norm{S^{\varepsilon}_{t,a}}_{2\to2}
 \le e^{(D_u+\kappa/N)(a-t)}.
\]
For each fixed singular path, its minimum separation on a finite horizon is
positive. Local $C^1$ convergence of the regularized force and a stopped
Gronwall argument give uniform path convergence. Testing first on continuous
terminal functions passes the Haar measure domination to the singular process.
Extension to Borel measures and density of continuous functions give the same
$L^2$ bound and strong, fixed-parameter convergence of the semigroups.
No operator-norm convergence or convergence of regularized source potentials
is inferred from this terminal-data argument.

\section*{Restoring both responses}
The finite-measure response is
\begin{align*}
 \R_{x,t}\Phi(x,y)
 ={}&-\int\mu_t(x+w)\Phi(x+w,y)\,D_K(dw)\\
    &-\int K(w)\cdot\nabla\mu_t(x+w)\Phi(x+w,y)\,dw.
\end{align*}
The second slot is analogous. Each has Borel supremum and Haar $L^2$ norm
at most $M_0\norm{D_K}_{\rm TV}+M_1\norm K_1$. At Coulomb,
\[
 D_K=c_d(\delta_0-dx),\qquad c_d=(d-2)|\mathbb S^{d-1}|.
\]
The atom contributes $-c_d\mu_t(x)\Phi(x,y)$, not a trace on $x=y$.
Off the diagonal, changing diagonal values changes the integral only at
$w=y-x\ne0$, where neither $D_K$ nor the absolutely continuous $K$ term
has an atom. Thus the off-diagonal Borel operator is well defined. Haar
equivalence-class independence follows separately by translation and Fubini.

Put $C_R=2(M_0\norm{D_K}_{\rm TV}+M_1\norm K_1)$ and
$c=D_u+\kappa/2$. For
\[
 (Vh)_t=\int_t^T S_{t,a}(\R_{x,a}+\R_{y,a})h_a\,da,
 \qquad \Phi=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}V^jU_N,
\]
the ordered time simplex gives a Borel supremum bound
$\norm{U_N}_\infty(C_RT)^j/j!$. The intervening semigroup exponents add
over adjacent time intervals, so the $L^2$ bound is
$e^{cT}\sup_a\norm{U_N(a)}_2(C_RT)^j/j!$. Both series converge consistently;
the same factorial estimate proves uniqueness in the two separate classes.
In particular
\[
 \sup_t\norm{\Phi_t}_2\le e^{(c+C_R)T}\sup_t\norm{U_N(t)}_2.
\]
Conditional Markov time splitting identifies the solution with the true
uniformly integrable martingale whose integrated source is
$J+(\R_x+\R_y)\Phi$. Absolute occupation integrability justifies the time
interchanges. This is a probabilistic inverse; it is not a singular It\^o
formula for the $N$-particle corrector.

\section*{The initial endpoint and an actual homogeneous subcase}
For iid initial law $\mu_0$, retain the exact mean-field-centered statistic
$P_N=U_2/2$ with denominator $N^2$. THM--015 gives, with
$b_N=\min(\beta_N,1)$,
\begin{align*}
 \E\left|\sqrt{Nb_N}P_N[\Phi_0]\right|^2
 &\le\frac{b_N}{2N}\norm{\Phi_0}_{L^2(\mu_0^2)}^2\\
 &\le\frac{b_NM_0^2}{2N}e^{2(c+C_R)T}C\rho_N.
\end{align*}
The factor is $M_0^2$: the full kernel need not depend only on $x-y$.
The deterministic mean and first projection have not been omitted. The rates
are $b_N/N$, $b_N(1+\log N)/N$, and
$b_NN^{-(d+2-s)/(s+2)}$, respectively. These all vanish in the declared range.

For actual homogeneous data, take $b=0$, $\mu_0=1$. Then $\mu_t=1$, $u_t=0$
solve the reference equation. For a real smooth terminal test $h$, set
\[
 f_t(x)=\widehat h(0)+\sum_{k\ne0}\widehat h(k)
 e^{-(T-t)a_k(\nu)}e^{2\pi i k\cdot x},\qquad
 a_k(\nu)=4\pi^2\nu|k|^2+4\pi^2c_{d,s}|k|^{s+2-d}.
\]
All $a_k$ are positive. Rapid Fourier decay justifies time and spatial
differentiation and bounds every fixed spatial derivative uniformly for
$t\in[0,T]$ and $\nu\ge0$. The one-body response multiplier is
$-4\pi^2|k|^2\widehat g_s(k)$; hence
$\partial_tf+\nu\Delta f+\R f=0$ with $f_T=h$.
This verifies the required background and test norms for this explicit
actual-data subcase. The pair estimate still requires bounded $\nu$.

With $\nu=1/\beta_N$, the conclusion therefore covers
$\beta_N\ge\beta_*>0$. It includes every microscopically critical sequence
$\lambda_N=\beta_NN^{s/d-1}\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)$, which eventually has
$\beta_N\to\infty$, and subcritical sequences satisfying the lower bound.
Subcritical sequences with $\beta_N\to0$ are not covered. The old condition
$\beta_NN^{2s/d-1}\to0$ remains distinct from microscopic subcriticality.
