\paragraph{Model and scope.}
Fix $d\ge3$, $0<s\le d-2$ and finite $T\ge0$. On the mass-one torus, initial particles are iid Haar and independent of their independent Brownian drivers. The actual noncolliding singular dynamics is
\[
 dX_i=\frac1N\sum_{\ell\ne i}K(X_i-X_\ell)\,dt+\sqrt{2\nu}\,dW_i,
 \qquad K=-\nabla g,
 \qquad \widehat g(k)=c_{d,s}|k|^{s-d}\quad(k\ne0),
\]
where $\widehat g(0)=0$ and
$c_{d,s}=\pi^{s-d/2}\Gamma((d-s)/2)/\Gamma(s/2)$ for the Fourier convention $e^{2\pi i k\cdot x}$.
Set $b(0)=1$, $b(\nu)=\min(1,\nu^{-1})$ for $\nu>0$, and
$\rho_N=\eta_N-dx$. Thus $b\le1$ and $\nu b\le1$ for every finite diffusivity.
Common translations and pathwise uniqueness preserve the initial translation invariance. Every one-body marginal is exactly Haar, so this centering equals exact first-marginal centering; higher marginals remain correlated.

For the law statements add $s<d/2$ and fix a finite list of deterministic times and real smooth tests $(t_j,h_j)_{j=1}^m$.
Let $Q_t^\nu$ preserve constants and have nonzero multiplier $e^{-tL_k(\nu)}$, with
\[
 a_k=4\pi^2|k|^2,\qquad
 D_k=4\pi^2c_{d,s}|k|^{s+2-d},\qquad L_k(\nu)=D_k+\nu a_k>0.
\]
The covariance is the sum of two Gram matrices:
\begin{align*}
 C_{ij}(\nu)={}&b\int(Q_{t_i}^\nu h_i-\textstyle\int h_i)
                        (Q_{t_j}^\nu h_j-\textstyle\int h_j)\,dx\\
 &+2\nu b\int_0^{t_i\wedge t_j}\int
             \nabla Q_{t_i-r}^\nu h_i\cdot\nabla Q_{t_j-r}^\nu h_j\,dx\,dr.
\end{align*}
Equivalently, the exact modal weights are
\[
 C_{ij}(\nu)=b\sum_{k\ne0}\widehat h_i(k)\overline{\widehat h_j(k)}
 \left[\frac{D_k}{L_k}e^{-(t_i+t_j)L_k}
       +\frac{\nu a_k}{L_k}e^{-|t_i-t_j|L_k}\right].
\]
The initial component has time-sum damping; the thermal component also contains the time difference. Constants, zero/repeated times and singular covariance are included.

\paragraph{The two accepted assertions.}

\[ Z_{N,j}=\sqrt{Nb(\nu_N)}\,\rho_N(t_j)[h_j]. \]

where the distance is the supremum over real functions of sup norm at most one and Lipschitz constant at most one. The supremum is for a fixed finite time/test list. Infinity is never a coefficient in the particle equation.

\paragraph{Source uniformity and the singular identity.}
The original statistic is
\[

       -\eta_N\left[\int J(\cdot,y)\,dy\right]+\frac12\iint J.
\]

$D=(d-2)|\mathbb S^{d-1}|(\delta_0-dx)$; both atom and compensation remain.

\paragraph{The joint probability argument.}
Positive heat retention and the actual energy sign give
$\E|\widehat\rho_N(k)|^2\le\min(1,CN^{-(1-s/d)}|k|^{d-s})$ uniformly in all finite $\nu$. Absolute Fourier summation therefore controls the first absolute moment of every smooth gradient-product bracket test. For a fixed real vector $u$, let $M=u\cdot M_{N,\nu}$, $q=\langle M\rangle_T$ and let $v$ be its deterministic Haar bracket. Then
\[
 \E|q-v|\le C_uN^{-(1-s/d)/2},\qquad 0\le q,v\le K_u,
\]
uniformly in $\nu$, because $\nu b\le1$. The complex exponential
$\exp(iM_t+\langle M\rangle_t/2)$ is a true martingale with conditional expectation one given the initial sigma field. Multiplying by $e^{iu\cdot A}$ and replacing its bounded compensator by $v$ costs at most $C_u\E|q-v|$. This proves the needed asymptotic factorization without finite-$N$ independence.

The initial bounded iid triangular summands give a uniform characteristic-function error $O_u(N^{-1/2})$. Gaussian convolution, uniform second-moment tails for $A+M$, and the $L^1$ source remainder prove the full bounded-Lipschitz supremum. Removing the smoothing by a Lipschitz coupling includes degenerate covariances.
