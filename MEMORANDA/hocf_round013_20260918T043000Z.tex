\section*{Statement and fixed conventions}
Fix $d\ge4$, $0<s<2$, $0\le T,\nu_*<\infty$ and a smooth real terminal test
$h$. Use the unit Haar torus, Fourier characters $e^{2\pi i k\cdot x}$,
coefficient-one periodic Riesz potential $g$, and $K=-\nabla g$. The actual
particles start iid Haar independently of their Brownian drivers and satisfy
\[
 \dd X_i=\frac1N\sum_{j\ne i}K(X_i-X_j)\dd t+\sqrt{2\nu}\dd W_i.
\]
There is no external drift. The reference remains Haar. Let $\Phi_t$ be the
genuine symmetric terminal-zero full pair inverse with both response slots
and the source $J_t(x,y)=K(x-y)\cdot(\nabla f_t(x)-\nabla f_t(y))$, where
$f$ is the actual homogeneous Fourier backward test. Set
\[
 p=s+2,\qquad a=s/p,\qquad\theta=1-s/d,\qquad
 b=\min(1,\nu^{-1}),\qquad\sigma_N^2=Nb\quad(\nu>0).
\]
The corrector is the literal ordered deleted-pair statistic
\[
 P_N[\Phi]=\frac1{2N^2}\sum_{i\ne j}\Phi(X_i,X_j)
 -\frac1N\sum_i\int\Phi(X_i,y)\dd y+\frac12\int\Phi(x,y)\dd x\dd y.
\]
Its actual scaled expected martingale bracket is
\[
 Q_N=2\nu Nb\,\E\int_0^T\sum_i|\nabla_iP_N[\Phi_t](X_t)|^2\dd t.
\]
At zero noise define $Q_N=0$ directly. Uniformly in every $N\ge2$ and every
$0<\nu\le\nu_*$,
\[
 Q_N\le Cb\min(N^a,\nu^{-s/2})(N^{-\theta}+\nu).
\]
Moreover the entire bounded interval satisfies
\[
 \sup_{0\le\nu\le\nu_*}Q_N\longrightarrow0,
\]
with the sufficient uniform rate
\[
 Q_N\le C\left[N^{a-\theta}+N^{-(2-s)/(12p^2)}
 +N^{-s/(4p(s+4))}+N^{-1/(2p)}+N^{-2/p}\right].
\]
Constants depend only on the fixed displayed data and weights. No limiting
or boundedness assumption on $\nu N^{2/p}$ is made.

\section*{Two occupation estimates for the genuine derivative}
Use the fixed periodic multiplicative weights $w_q=w_1^q$, with
$w_q(z)=|z|^{-q}$ near zero and $w_1\ge1$. For each fixed
\[
 1<q<\min(d-2,d/2),\qquad q\le s+1,\qquad\eta=s+1-q<2,
\]
the full inverse satisfies
\[
 \sup_t\sup_{x\ne y}\frac{|\nabla_{x,y}\Phi_t(x,y)|}{w_q(x-y)}
 \le C_q\min(N^{\eta/p},\nu^{-\eta/2}).
\]
Write $\mathcal G=\nu\Delta_{x,y}+N^{-1}K(x-y)\cdot(\nabla_x-\nabla_y)$.
The largest transverse eigenvalue of its drift Jacobian is
$2s|x-y|^{-p}/N$; the radial eigenvalue is negative. Let $\ell$ be this
nonnegative upper bound with the fixed smooth remainder included.
For a fixed inner radius $R$, direct differentiation gives
\[
 (\mathcal G+\ell)w_q\le C_qw_q
 -2q(d-2-q)\nu\,\mathbf1_{\{r\le R\}}w_{q+2}
 -\frac{2s(q-1)}N\mathbf1_{\{r\le R\}}w_{q+p}.
\]
Both coefficients are strictly favorable. Annular errors cost a fixed
constant because diffusivity is bounded. Stopped nonnegative weighted
It\^o inequalities give a terminal bound and both occupation bounds, with
coefficients $\nu$ and $1/N$. Noncollision, Fatou and monotone convergence
remove the stops at each fixed $N,\nu$.

The source gradient has weight $w_{s+1}$. Dividing by $w_q$ and using either
occupation moment bounds its time-path integral by $CN^{\eta/p}w_q$ or
$C\nu^{-\eta/2}w_q$. Fixed-$N$ higher moments and common local starts from
the earlier derivative theorem justify differentiation of expectation
\emph{before} these improved uniform estimates are applied. The old
constants are not relabeled uniform.

With $D=\operatorname{div}K$, the two homogeneous responses are
\[
 \mathcal RF=-\int F(x+w,y)D(\dd w)-\int F(x,y+w)D(\dd w).
\]
Their weak derivatives differentiate $F$, not the singular measure.
Separated-singularity convolution gives a bound in the derivative seminorm
alone; there is no value-amplitude term. The full differentiated Volterra
series has a factorial tail and identifies the same bounded Borel inverse.
Equivalently, Gronwall applies to that existing finite-$N$ inverse.

\section*{Actual higher-power occupation}
For $H_N=N^{-1}\sum_{i<j}g(X_i-X_j)$, the sharp heat-integral floor is
$H_N\ge-CN^{s/d}$. Strictly below Coulomb,
$D_{\rm cl}\ge c w_p-C$ with $c>0$, and $p<d$. The exact actual energy
identity, initially of mean zero, retains the whole force square and the
coefficient $\nu(N-1)$ on pair-divergence occupation. Hence
\[
 \nu\E\int_0^T w_p(X_1-X_2)\dd t\le C(N^{-\theta}+\nu).
\]
No individual pair-force square is extracted from the total-force square.
The background compensation contributes the retained $C\nu$ term.

\section*{From the exact square to the uniform singular tail}
Set $G=\nabla_x\Phi$ and $A(x)=\int G(x,y)\dd y$. Literal differentiation
of the ordered statistic yields
\[
 v_i=\nabla_iP_N=N^{-2}\left[\sum_{j\ne i}G(X_i,X_j)-NA(X_i)\right].
\]
The subtraction is $N A$, not $(N-1)A$. For any field $F$ with its own row,
exchangeability, the actual one-body Haar law and finite-sum
Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 Q_N[F]\le4\nu b\int_0^T\left[
 \frac{(N-1)^2}{N^2}\E|F(X_1,X_2)|^2+\|F\|_{L^2(\mathrm{Haar}^2)}^2
 \right]\dd t.
\]
This bounds the complete nonnegative square; no signed triple term is
discarded. With $q=p/2$, the derivative estimate gives
$|G|^2\le C\min(N^a,\nu^{-s/2})w_p$, proving the direct bound for $Q_N$.
For $0<\nu\le N^{-\epsilon}$ it implies
\[
 Q_N\le C[N^{a-\theta}+N^{-\epsilon(1-s/2)}].
\]

For the remaining interval choose
\[
 q_-=1+s/4,\qquad\eta_-=3s/4,\qquad
 r=p/q_-=\frac{4(s+2)}{s+4}>2.
\]
Radially clip $G$ at magnitude $L$, writing $G=G^L+R_L$, and recompute the
Haar row for each field. Since $w_{q_-}^r=w_p$,
\[
 |R_L|^2\le L^{2-r}|G|^r
 \le C\nu^{-\eta_-r/2}L^{2-r}w_p.
\]
The actual occupation therefore yields the residual bracket bound
\[
 \mathcal T_N(L)\le Cb\nu^{-\eta_-r/2}L^{2-r}(N^{-\theta}+\nu).
\]
The physical $\nu$ is already included in the occupation estimate.
No additional inverse-noise factor is needed.
The separately accepted R10 actual clipping estimate, at
$L_N=N^{1/(4p)}$, gives
\[
 Q_N[G^{L_N}]\le C(N^{-2/p}+N^{-1/(2p)}).
\]
The probability-time-particle Hilbert triangle inequality gives
$Q_N\le2Q_N[G^{L_N}]+2\mathcal T_N(L_N)$, without orthogonality.
Choose $\epsilon=1/(6p^2)$. On $\nu\ge N^{-\epsilon}$ the tail exponent is
\[
 \epsilon\eta_-r/2-(r-2)/(4p)=-s/[4p(s+4)].
\]
Combining both intervals proves the stated rate. Since
$a-\theta=(s(s+2)-2d)/(dp)<0$ for $d\ge4,0<s<2$, every term decays.

The leading scaled bracket is bounded by $2T\sup\|\nabla f\|_\infty^2$.
The expected integral of the absolute scaled cross-variation is bounded
by $C\sqrt{Q_N}$ and also vanishes uniformly. No dimension-three, $s=2$,
Coulomb, logarithmic, general-background or unbounded-diffusivity extension
is inferred. The old condition $\beta_NN^{2s/d-1}\to0$, full subcriticality
$\lambda_N=\beta_NN^{s/d-1}\to0$, and positive finite criticality remain
distinct. The actual cubic drift is the next open assertion.
