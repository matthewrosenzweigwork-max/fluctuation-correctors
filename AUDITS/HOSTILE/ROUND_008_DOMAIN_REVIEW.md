## Analytic stress tests

### Weighted barriers and arbitrary compact-start moments

I recomputed the principal pair Jacobian in product Euclidean coordinates. The center directions have eigenvalue zero. On relative directions the transverse eigenvalue is `a r^(-p)` and the radial eigenvalue is `-(s+1)a r^(-p)`, where `a=2s/N` and `p=s+2`. Therefore the candidate uses the correct largest symmetric eigenvalue. Substituting the absolute Hessian norm would incorrectly replace the singular coefficient by `(s+1)a`.

For `w_alpha=r^(-alpha)` in the local chart, direct product-space differentiation gives

    (G+m*c)w_alpha
      = -a(alpha-m) r^(-alpha-p)
        + 2nu*alpha(alpha+2-d) r^(-alpha-2)
        - (2alpha/N) r^(-alpha-2) k(z).z.

The smooth remainder is bounded by `(2alpha L_k/N)w_alpha`. With `A=a(alpha-m)>0`, the maximum of `D*u^2-(A/2)*u^(s+2)` occurs at `u^s=4D/[A(s+2)]`, and its value is precisely the displayed `sD/(s+2)` times that power raised to `2/s`. This absorbs positive diffusion even when alpha exceeds the harmonic exponent. The annular supremum includes the cutoff drift, cutoff Laplacian, exponential weight, and occupation weight. Hence neither the Coulomb endpoint nor positive large moment exponents invalidate the barrier.

Stopped smooth Itô is applied before passing expectations. Its local stochastic integrands are bounded; Fatou handles the nonnegative terminal quantity and monotone convergence handles occupation. The resulting nonnegative local supermartingale has the stated maximal tail estimate. For any required finite moment Q, choosing a larger R, then `m=lR` and `alpha>max(m,bR)`, yields the compact-start moment for the time supremum of `exp(lI)w_b`. The resulting constant may be very large and depend on N. There is no illicit all-N bound.

### Common-start flow and expectation derivatives

The delicate point in the common-completeness argument is behavior at bad initial states. It is valid here. If good initial points approaching a bad point had bounded pathwise inverse-separation supremum, all those paths would lie in a single collision-excluded compact set. A single smooth truncation would agree with them through the horizon. Its continuous dependence would produce a separated limiting path from the alleged bad point, a contradiction. Thus the inverse-separation supremum tends to infinity at every bad point, and its cap at n is locally constant near that point.

On the good open set the capped supremum is locally Lipschitz by compact-time flow differentiation. Its gradient is bounded almost everywhere by the displayed supremum of `exp(I)w_2`. Per-start noncollision and joint measurability make the bad set Lebesgue null almost surely; Fubini and the high-moment bound give uniform expected W1Q norms of all caps on an initial ball with closure in E. The radial-segment Sobolev calculation is valid for Q greater than the initial-space dimension 2d. Applied on a compact inner ball, it bounds the Qth moment of each capped supremum uniformly in n. Monotone convergence then makes the uncapped compact supremum finite almost surely. Countably many inner balls give one full-probability event covering all off-diagonal starts for the fixed parameter tuple.

This proves exactly the common-start assertion needed for deterministic initial segments. It does not claim a common exceptional set for uncountably many noise coefficients, and the candidate explicitly avoids that claim.

The second variation solves the stated inhomogeneous linear ODE. Its norm is bounded by a constant times `exp(2I_T) sup w_(s+3)`. Because weights are at least one and I is nondecreasing, that is bounded by the square of a single weighted time supremum, whose arbitrary moments have just been proved. The first variation has the corresponding exponential bound. Multiplying by any fixed inverse-distance power remains integrable to a power greater than one on compact initial sets.

Consequently, first and second chain-rule expressions converge almost surely along convergent starts and are uniformly integrable. Their expectations are continuous. Fubini on a deterministic short initial segment is justified by the moment bound, and averaging the segment fundamental theorem gives both derivatives of the expectation. The same majorants justify source time integration and continuity as the source interval shrinks to zero. This is stronger than pathwise differentiability at a single start and supplies the missing expectation argument rather than naming it.

### Classical equation and the internal-drift cancellation

Once C1-time/C2-space regularity has been proved, local smooth Itô is applicable to the auxiliary pair process on a compact subset of E. ## Particle algebra, partial backgrounds, and the stochastic passage

Let `p(x,y)=grad_1 Phi(x,y)`, `q(x)=integral Phi(x,y)dy`, `a=grad q`, and `r=integral Phi`. Uniform derivative-weight integrals and a moving-tube split make the differentiated contractions continuous. The candidate then identifies them as weak derivatives and hence classical derivatives of q. In particular q is C1 in time and C2 in space, with bounded second derivative. The slice integral of the second-slot Laplacian is zero: its deleted-sphere flux is of order `epsilon^(d-1-q1)`.

For the raw triple term `A(x,y,z)=K(x-z).p(x,y)`, integrate conditional on x. The force has exponent s+1 less than d in the x-z variable; the gradient has exponent q1 less than d in the separate x-y variable. Their product is integrable in the full triple space, including simultaneous triple approach. For two fixed distinct empirical points, each one-background permutation has only two separated singular locations, so its integral is absolutely finite. With one empirical point and two backgrounds the conditional-on-x estimate is uniform. Coinciding background coordinates are null sets; two equal empirical labels never appear, and distinct empirical coordinates remain distinct almost surely. No partial-diagonal trace is required.

I expanded the six permutations before integrating. They give

    C1(x,y) = [K(x-y).(a(x)-a(y)) + (R_x+R_y)Phi(x,y)]/6,
    C2(x)   = v(x)/3,
    C0      = 0,
    v(x)    = integral K(z-x).a(z)dz.

The response contraction is exactly v and its scalar average is zero. These identities are justified by the absolute integrals just described. The order-one force contraction involving a is also harmless because a is bounded and Lipschitz.

Now start with the actual observable

    P = (1/(2N^2)) sum_(i!=j) Phi(x_i,x_j)
        - (1/N) sum_i q(x_i) + r/2.

Its particle-force contribution on a collision-excluded compact configuration set is

    Q = N^(-3) sum_(i!=j) sum_(k!=i) K(x_i-x_k).p(x_i,x_j)
        - N^(-2) sum_(i!=k) K(x_i-x_k).a(x_i).

Split the first sum into k=j and three distinct labels. Pairing the two orientations of k=j gives exactly `D2[B Phi]/(2N)`; the three-distinct part is the ordered sum of the symmetrized triple kernel. Expanding U3 by its literal slot-subset definition and inserting the above contractions gives

    Q = U3[C Phi] + P[R Phi] + D2[B Phi]/(2N).

This proves the upward coefficient one and both response slots independently of a full-product diagonal formula. The algebraic recentering is

    D2[B Phi] - 2P[B Phi]
      = 2rho[(B Phi)_mu] + integral B Phi.

Combining it with the classical corrector equation yields the exact displayed THM028 drift, including `1/N` and `1/(2N)`. In particular, the background terms in U3 generally survive at N=2 even though its all-distinct triple sum is empty.

Differentiation of the same literal observable gives

    grad_i P = N^(-2) sum_(j!=i) p(x_i,x_j) - a(x_i)/N.

Independent particle noises therefore give the stochastic integral in the statement and exactly `2nu sum_i |grad_i P|^2 dt` as quadratic variation. Expanding that square retains both the repeated neighbor label and the all-distinct neighbor labels, as well as both mixed background terms. There is no additional thermal trace: the two labels in each particle-particle observable are distinct, and the integrated background Laplacians vanish by the slice proof. At zero noise both martingale and bracket vanish.

The fixed-N law domination from R6 transfers each proved Haar-integrable drift term to a finite absolute expected time integral. It also transfers the squared gradient: finite-sum Cauchy–Schwarz gives the candidate's sufficient bound

    sum_i ||grad_i P||_2^2
       <= 2(N-1)^2 ||p||_2^2/N^3 + 2||a||_2^2/N,

and `||a||_2<=||p||_2`. The gradient is L2 because `2q1<d`. Multiplication by `2nu_* T ||F0||infinity exp((N-1)kappa T)` bounds the full expected bracket. Thus this is a square-integrable true martingale, not only a localized martingale.

On each realized finite-horizon particle path the minimum separation is positive. The collision stops therefore exhaust the whole horizon. The bounded observable and its undifferentiated contractions converge in L2. Grouped drift integrals converge in L1 by absolute expected integrability, and the stochastic integrals converge in L2 by Itô isometry. The identity follows at deterministic times and then simultaneously through continuity and a countable dense set. Unsymmetrized repeated-pair terms need not separately be integrable: the proof correctly pairs them before the final estimates. It does not pass a divergent term separately or evaluate a singular diagonal.
