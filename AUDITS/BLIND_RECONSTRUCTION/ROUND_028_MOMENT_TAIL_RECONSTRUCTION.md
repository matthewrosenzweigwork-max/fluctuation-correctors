## 3. The compact-abelian four-edge identity

Let \(G\) be **any** compact abelian group with Haar probability; a bounded Haar-measurable real even zero-mean \(h\) suffices. Define convolution by
\((h*h)(z)=\int_G h(z-y)h(y)dy\).
All products below are integrable by boundedness and Haar invariance. Expand
\((\sum_{i<j}h(Y_i-Y_j))^4\) as ordered lists of four unordered edges. If a label has degree one, integrate that label first: its single incident Haar row is zero. Thus only the following leaf-free multigraphs survive:

| Used labels | Edge multiplicities/shape | Count on each fixed label set | Integral |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 2 | one edge four times | 1 | \(\mu_4\) |
| 3 | two different edges twice each | \(3\cdot6=18\) | \(\mu_2^2\) |
| 3 | triangle with one edge doubled | \(3\cdot12=36\) | \(\tau\) |
| 4 | two disjoint doubled edges | \(3\cdot6=18\) | \(\mu_2^2\) |
| 4 | a four-cycle | \(3\cdot24=72\) | \(\chi\) |

There can be no fifth used label, since total degree is eight and surviving degrees are at least two. These shapes exhaust two, three and four labels. For the doubled path, condition on its middle variable and integrate the two independent outer Haar variables, producing \(\mu_2^2\). For a triangle set one vertex to zero by common translation: the two single edges integrate to (h*h), and the doubled edge gives \(\int h^2(h*h)=\tau\); evenness fixes all orientations. For a four-cycle, integrate alternating vertices: the remaining integral is \(\int(h*h)^2=\chi\). These arguments need neither connectedness nor absence of torsion nor a countable dual group.

Since \(H^h=N^{-1}\sum_{i<j}h(Y_i-Y_j)\), multiplication by \(N^{-4}\) now proves exactly
\[
N^4\mathbb E(H^h)^4={N\choose2}\mu_4+
18{N\choose3}\mu_2^2+36{N\choose3}\tau+
18{N\choose4}\mu_2^2+72{N\choose4}\chi.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Vanishing binomial coefficients handle every small \(N\). The formula would generally fail without centering, since degree-one graphs would no longer vanish. No pairwise-independence assertion has been made about overlapping edges. Cauchy--Schwarz in the convolution gives
\[
