## 1. Frozen regime convention

For positive-power Riesz interaction \(0<s<d\), define
\[
\lambda_N:=\beta_NN^{s/d-1}.
\]
Use the following terminology throughout:

### 1.1 Existing energy-floor range

The current generic pathwise closure imposes
\[
\beta_NN^{2s/d-1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Equivalently, the fluctuation normalization times the completed-energy floor is forced to vanish in the thermal regime. This is the restriction to be superseded, not silently renamed.

### 1.2 Full microscopically subcritical regime

\[
\lambda_N\longrightarrow0,
\qquad	ext{equivalently}\qquad
\beta_N\ll N^{1-s/d}.
\]
The campaign must allow arbitrarily slow convergence of \(\lambda_N\) to zero. A theorem that assumes a hidden polynomial gap, a second small parameter, or the old energy-floor condition is not the flagship theorem.

### 1.3 Microscopically critical regime

\[
\lambda_N\longrightarrow\lambda\in(0,\infty),
\qquad	ext{equivalently}\qquad
\beta_N\sim\lambda N^{1-s/d}.
\]
At critical coupling, the campaign must determine whether a finite corrector truncation closes. If not, construct the required resummation or enlarged state and prove its convergence.

### 1.4 Supercritical regime

\[
\lambda_N\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
This is not the principal mission, but it is an obligatory falsification and sharpness regime. Use it to identify where the critical theory must fail or change character.

### 1.5 Logarithmic interaction

Maintain a separate logarithmic scaling ledger. Do not substitute \(s=0\) into positive-power formulas without proving continuity of every normalization, diagonal counterterm, and local free-energy coefficient.

## 3. Exact finite-\(N\) object to resolve

The first-order adjoint-duality identity contains a time-integrated quadratic commutator. The old proof bounds it by completed modulated energy. The new theory must **incorporate rather than discard** it.

For a terminal test function \(\phi\), let \(f^{r,t}_\phi\) be the backward solution for the linearized mean-field dynamics. The finite-\(N\) identity has the structural form
\[
\langle \phi,\rho_N^t\rangle
=
\langle f^{0,t}_\phi,\rho_N^0\rangle
+
\mathcal M_{N,t}(f)
+
\frac12\int_0^t\operatorname{Comm}_N(M^*\nabla f^{r,t}_\phi)\,dr.
\]
The campaign must derive this identity afresh under the frozen normalization and then replace the last term by exact corrector dynamics.

### 3.1 Quadratic corrector

For a symmetric two-body kernel \(\Phi_2^r\), define the deleted-diagonal quadratic statistic with all normalization factors explicit. Apply the exact particle generator and mean-field evolution. The result must display:

- the full two-body linearized operator, including every background-response term generated by expanding the empirical measure;
- the nonlinear coupling to the particle force;
- Itô trace/contraction terms;
- diagonal counterterms;
- the martingale and its quadratic variation;
- regularization-dependent terms for singular kernels.

Do not solve only independent transport in the two variables if the exact expansion contains additional quadratic terms. Define the two-body operator as the exact coefficient of the quadratic fluctuation after all linear cancellations, then identify its analytic form.

### 3.2 Higher-order hierarchy

For every \(k\ge2\), define a canonical deleted-diagonal, centered \(k\)-body statistic \(\mathcal U_{N,k}[\Phi_k]\). Derive the exact generator recursion. At minimum it must resolve:

- the \(k\)-body transport/linearized operator;
- upward couplings \(k\to k+1\) from the nonlinear force;
- downward contractions \(k\to k-1\) and \(k\to k-2\) from diagonal coincidences and Itô calculus;
- deterministic counterterms;
- martingale terms and cross-variations;
- finite-\(N\) combinatorial coefficients;
- symmetry factors and deleted-diagonal conventions.

Manually verify \(k=2\) and \(k=3\). Use symbolic combinatorial verification for \(k=4\) and a general recursion. The discovery implementation and independent verifier must not share substantive algorithms.

### 3.3 Corrected observable

Derive—not guess—the appropriate corrected fluctuation observable. Its generic architecture may be
\[
\mathcal Z_{N,t}^{[K]}(\phi)
=
\sigma_N\langle f^{t,t}_\phi,\rho_N^t\rangle
+
\sum_{k=2}^{K}\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathfrak G_k}
 a_{N,\Gamma}\Big(
 \mathcal U_{N,k}[\Phi_{k,\Gamma}^t]-c_{N,k,\Gamma}^t

but no coefficient, graph class, counterterm, or truncation order is frozen in advance. Determine them from the exact hierarchy and scaling analysis.

A candidate route is automatically blocked or demoted if it relies on any of the following without a new proof:

1. pointwise nonnegative weights implying positivity of a weighted Riesz quadratic form;
2. a Taylor remainder being called “less singular” when its leading coefficient retains the original singularity;
3. subtracting a crystalline/local-equilibrium energy and then treating the residual as coercive;
4. replacing control of a centered random commutator by control of its mean;
5. replacing exact first-marginal centering by an instantaneous Gibbs marginal;
6. using equilibrium expansion coefficients at the initial iid law without a bridge;
7. omitting the diagonal contribution of \(\Delta g\) in singular Itô calculus;
8. dropping a corrector martingale or its cross-variation;
9. assuming conditional rotation invariance after conditioning on an exterior configuration;
10. using scalar energy control to bound traceless stress without an isotropy theorem;
11. assuming fast microscopic relaxation from \(\lambda_N\to0\) when its time scale has not been compared with macroscopic time;
12. treating a periodic kernel globally as \(|x|^{-s}\) or using a global Euclidean midpoint on the torus;
13. inferring a field theorem from finite-dimensional convergence without tightness;
14. inferring a critical theorem from a fixed-order expansion without uniform remainder control;
15. hiding temperature dependence in constants;
16. proving a theorem for exact centering and advertising mean-field centering;
17. claiming an all-\(s\) formula by analytic continuation across the Hilbert–Schmidt threshold;
18. using a source theorem outside its geometry, law class, or normalization.

Record every encounter with these traps in `APPROACHES/OBSTRUCTION_LEDGER.md`.
